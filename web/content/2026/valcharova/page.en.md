<h1>AD&Eacute;LA VALCHAŘOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Welcome to Velveton</h2>
<p><em>Painting Studio 1</em></p>
<p><strong>What is &ldquo;Velveton&rdquo; in the name of your project? What did your graduate&nbsp;work focus on?</strong><br>The individual paintings comprise fragments of a fictitious town that I have&nbsp;called Velveton. Velvet is a material that evokes softness and smoothness,&nbsp;which I wanted to reflect in the technical execution of the painting. From&nbsp;the very outset, I sought to depict a soft, visually calming surface without&nbsp;any expressive strokes. I wanted it to feel smooth and pleasant. Velveton&nbsp;also invokes something constructed, almost artificial, which has to do with&nbsp;the character of the depicted motifs. These are built on direct geometrical&nbsp;lines that give the individual features an architectural, structured feel.&nbsp;I try to evoke feelings of solitude and emptiness, but without any sense&nbsp;of discomfort.</p>
<p><strong>Does your abstract painting find inspiration in the real world?</strong><br>Yes, I find nature very inspiring. The paintings feature animal motifs, for&nbsp;example birds or cows, as well as landscape features such as a tree, the&nbsp;Moon, an empty steppe or a wall of ice.</p>
<p><br><strong>The paintings are smooth and geometrical. They exude tranquility. Was&nbsp;this tranquility always part of your work or did you have to find your own&nbsp;path to it?</strong><br>The sense of tranquility has to do with the geometric features I use, and&nbsp;I developed these gradually. It was a journey for me &ndash; I had to lose the sense&nbsp;of business that I often struggle with. It comes at me both from the outside&nbsp;world as well as from my own thinking. The paintings thus show a paradox&nbsp;in which I render organic, biological motifs into structured forms. Geometry&nbsp;thus allows me to create my own sense of order, and reducing things to&nbsp;simple surfaces creates the sense of tranquility I have mentioned.</p>
