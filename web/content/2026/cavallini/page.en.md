<h1>ELEONORA CAVALLINI</h1>
<h2>Re-belonging</h2>
<p><em>Fine Art and Design</em></p>
<p><strong>When you write about nature, you mention it is a &ldquo;non-judgmental space.&rdquo;&nbsp;Do you understand that as an alternative to social structures? Do you think&nbsp;the relationship to nature can also function as a form of resistance to the&nbsp;pressure of being constantly productive?</strong><br>Exactly &ndash; I perceived immersion in natural landscapes, away from urban&nbsp;environments, as a way to reconnect with a relational belonging beyond&nbsp;socially constructed frameworks. It becomes a space where you are less&nbsp;defined by judgement or expectation. In that sense, yes, the images and&nbsp;the interactions between my body and nature become a way of processing&nbsp;reality. Alongside a sense of slowing down &ndash; almost like a pause from the&nbsp;pressure of producing and doing.</p>
<p><strong>In your work, embroidery does not appear only as decoration, but rather&nbsp;as a form of feminine memory. Do you think about it as continuation of&nbsp;a collective history?</strong><br>I do. While working on the photographic series, I felt the feminist part&nbsp;missing. So, by thinking about how to build that connection, I realised the&nbsp;new generations are lacking knowledge on some practices once considered&nbsp;part of everyday life by our grandmothers. I thus asked both my grandma&nbsp;and the older generation around me to teach me their techniques, so I could&nbsp;contribute to keeping this shared heritage alive while paying tribute to&nbsp;women who used these crafts to escape the reality they were forced into.</p>
<p><strong>You said that belonging does not have to be connected to a specific geographical place. Does it mean that &ldquo;home&rdquo; can be connected to people,&nbsp;environment or even your own body?</strong><br>Of course, I believe that we are the home we decide to create around ourselves; whatever is created by people we care about, places which make&nbsp;us feel accepted and allow us to relax. I think the project is rather about&nbsp;how the body can interact with its surroundings to make you feel at home,&nbsp;more than feeling it in your body. I hope this makes sense. I believe we can&nbsp;all make sense of it in this way.</p>
