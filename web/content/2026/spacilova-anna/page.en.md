<h1>ANNA SP&Aacute;ČILOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Threshold</h2>
<p>Photography Studio<br><br><strong>Your graduate work consists of a video that includes an installation of found&nbsp;objects, and revolves around concepts of everyday life, routine, privacy,&nbsp;human closeness and care. What particular situation were you observing?</strong><br>I was observing my grandfather&rsquo;s everyday existence and where he lives. I was&nbsp;interested in his daily routine and how it works (or doesn&rsquo;t), as well as the&nbsp;relationships and situations developing around him. I was observing both him&nbsp;and his surroundings &ndash; my grandmother, who takes care of him; myself as an&nbsp;observer; small conversations and special moments; silence; objects left lying&nbsp;around; the atmosphere of the place. Everything that shapes and connects us.&nbsp;I focused on certain subtle situations that might seem insignificant or ordinary under normal circumstances but become strangely intense upon closer&nbsp;observation. That is why objects and materials transferred from their original&nbsp;environment also play a significant role. Hidden beneath a layer of plastic &ndash;&nbsp;touching, in direct contact, but not really.</p>
<p><strong>Is a personal connection to the situations and environments you explore&nbsp;essential to your work? Can you imagine working in an environment you don&rsquo;t&nbsp;know very well?</strong><br>A personal connection is important mainly because it allows me to stay in the&nbsp;given situation longer, is more natural, and I am more sensitive to it. I try to be&nbsp;present and capture the moment as it unfolds on its own. A personal setting&nbsp;also provides a unique blend of whimsy, humor, awkwardness, and sensitivity &ndash;&nbsp;this dynamic fascinates me.<br>But I don&rsquo;t think that this sense of threshold is tied solely to a personal or familiar environment. It can appear anywhere, as it depends more on the way one&nbsp;observes the situation and is open to it. I can imagine working in a completely&nbsp;unfamiliar space or with people I don&rsquo;t know. But perhaps there would arise&nbsp;a different kind of tension or a different kind of threshold.</p>
<p><strong>What exactly do you mean by the concept of &ldquo;threshold,&rdquo; which is so important to this work? Were you able to stick to your original intention during&nbsp;filming, or did the story lead you to unexpected places?</strong><br>It is that moment when something ordinary briefly becomes strange, intense,&nbsp;or elusive, without us knowing exactly why. It could be a detail, the atmosphere&nbsp;of a space, a peculiar sentence, or the situation&rsquo;s awkwardness.<br>My original vision evolved during the filming process. I initially wanted to&nbsp;capture the lightness and absurdity of everyday life, but the personal dimension of relationships gradually began to influence the work. Filming thus led&nbsp;me to the very threshold itself, that moment when lightness begins to turn&nbsp;heavy and one becomes more aware of their own sensitivity to the situation&nbsp;they are observing.</p>
