<h1>RICHARD MO&Scaron;TĚK</h1>
<h2>MURAL</h2>
<p><em>Painting Studio 3</em><br><br><strong>You decided to create your graduate work, which marks the completion&nbsp;of your studies in Brno, in the town of Jesen&iacute;k. What does the mural in&nbsp;a public space symbolize, and what is your relationship to the place?</strong><br>I chose Jesen&iacute;k deliberately; it is my hometown, where I grew up. So, the&nbsp;location is close to my heart, and I know it well. I conceived the mural&nbsp;thematically and visually as site-specific &ndash; taking into account both the&nbsp;geographical location of Jesen&iacute;k and the specific spot in the city where&nbsp;the mural is located. The mural itself explores the theme of water from&nbsp;various perspectives: from the local spa tradition of hydrotherapy, through&nbsp;its economic aspects, to the recurring floods in the region.</p>
<p><strong>Was it difficult to arrange for your work to be produced in the city? Who&nbsp;did you have to deal with, and how did the whole process go? How long&nbsp;can the painting remain on the building&rsquo;s facade?</strong><br>Since the Petr Bezruč Theater building is managed by the city, I first contacted the director of MKZ Jesen&iacute;k, Ms. Petra Fusov&aacute;, who trusted me and&nbsp;the project and played a major role in successfully navigating it through the&nbsp;city authorities, who eventually authorized it. According to the city plans,&nbsp;the theater&rsquo;s facade is scheduled for renovation as part of the Živel program&nbsp;in 2030 &ndash; so that is the provisional timeframe for the painting&rsquo;s lifespan.</p>
<p><strong>I read that the surface on which you created the painting had previously&nbsp;been used for advertising murals. Were you inspired by their aesthetic in&nbsp;any way, or did you want to completely transform the spirit of the place?&nbsp;Did you ever consider leaving some traces of the advertising layers beneath your painting?</strong><br>The previous advertisement had a strong 1990s typographic aesthetic.&nbsp;One of the initial impulses for choosing the location was to transform the&nbsp;advertising space into a non-commercial one. The advertisement that&nbsp;caught my attention most was the sign &ldquo;WELCOME TO JESEN&Iacute;K,&rdquo; which&nbsp;I could have imagined working with further, but I had doubts as to whether&nbsp;the general public would understand it.</p>
