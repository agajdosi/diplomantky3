<h1>ANNA TICH&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Landfills of the Present</h2>
<p><em>Body Design Studio</em><br><br><strong>Your work explores the issue of landfills. You see them not only as physical&nbsp;dumping grounds for waste, but also consider the landfills we find in the&nbsp;human psyche. What exactly do you mean by that?</strong><br>I use landfills, or dumps, as a motif of something that is an integral part of us&nbsp;and cannot simply be removed. They represent post-pain, embodying the&nbsp;experiences and sensations of other bodies and symbolizing the process&nbsp;of coming to terms with our (global) past. On an abstract level, they serve&nbsp;as a kind of emblem for the state in which we remain, an external image of&nbsp;our internal reality.</p>
<p><strong>What is the difference between a landfill and compost?</strong><br>I would rather focus on the difference between a landfill and composting,which I view in my work as both a metaphor and a material practice. According to theorist Donna J. Haraway, composting consists of the de- composition of old categories and structures, giving rise to new life from waste and crisis. It calls for shared survival and collaboration across species.&nbsp;<br>However, in this era of late-capitalist crisis, when this theory has not yet&nbsp;become global practice, I am still seeing just a hill of landfill instead of&nbsp;a compost pile. For me, the term dump is a metaphor for the physical and&nbsp;psychological environment on the planet, damaged by human activity.</p>
<p><strong>Your graduate work is quite monumental: what technique did you use&nbsp;and what materials does it consist of? What meanings do they convey?</strong><br>My work uses 90 kilograms of leftover warp threads which are waste&nbsp;materials from a factory that produces decorative and upholstery fabrics.&nbsp;I used sewing to create a nine-meter-long tapestry suspended in space.&nbsp;The material itself carries many lyrical meanings and layers for me; it is&nbsp;a symbol of constant reproduction, as well as a romantic object reflecting&nbsp;the present day.</p>
