<h1>KATEŘINA SVOBODOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Fragile Plant</h2>
<p><em>Painting Studio 3</em><br><br><strong>The central subject of your work <em>Křehkob&yacute;l</em> is the old Czech pastry&nbsp;called vrkoč. What exactly is it, and how do you reinterpret this object&nbsp;in your work?</strong><br>The vrkoč is a traditional Czech ceremonial pastry that symbolizes the sun,&nbsp;ritual gift-giving, and personal prayers for the future. It was not intended&nbsp;for consumption but served as a gift. It was imbued with magical powers.&nbsp;I am drawn to DIY aesthetics and the representation of spirituality in objects and images. That is why I tried to find a balance between simplicity&nbsp;and the mystery hidden within it, as well as the traditional form of the&nbsp;object. I suspect the very act of creating a ceremonial object can be a very&nbsp;powerful contemporary ritual. I have combined fabric with its symbolism&nbsp;of intertwining, small ornamental pastries, fire, and various uses of wax.&nbsp;The braid becomes part of the whole, together with the paintings and their&nbsp;garden of ceramic apples, which appear as the bearers of white light. The&nbsp;braid is a gift for the ancient and future Křehkob&yacute;l.</p>
<p><strong>Is ritual important to your work? The gifting of the braid has a certain&nbsp;ceremonial character.</strong><br>Absolutely. No matter what form of ritual we are talking about, I enjoy creating settings that can accurately convey a certain experience I have both&nbsp;during the creative process and outside of it &ndash; and this experience is inextricably linked to ritual. Gradually getting to know this charming, traditional&nbsp;object has guided me through a landscape I want to continue exploring.&nbsp;</p>
<p><strong>Your work features ruffled fabrics, beeswax, ceramics&hellip; What is your relationship to these materials?</strong><br>They fascinate me because they offer many ways to approach both the&nbsp;inner and outer environments. They facilitate deeper connection &ndash; both&nbsp;with oneself and with the experience of the surrounding world. I am just&nbsp;getting to know these materials, and it is important to me that they help me&nbsp;navigate through them and through myself, and that I can somehow better&nbsp;grasp them. The materials evoke a sense of calmness, which I also capture&nbsp;in my paintings. The white, sun-bleached and understated fabric steps out&nbsp;of the painting and into space. I felt as though I was painting, but with fabric!<br>The red ceramic apple towering at the top signifies the presence of the&nbsp;sun at the time of the winter solstice, a time when people needed spiritual&nbsp;forces more than at any other time of the year. Winter was harsh, cold, and&nbsp;full of fears about future survival. People would turn inward and call to&nbsp;the sun as living energy for the return of light. The experience of creating&nbsp;apples as a message of tomorrow&rsquo;s coming light was important to me. I hold&nbsp;beeswax and ceramics in high regard and perceive them very sensually and&nbsp;haptically. In my paintings, I really let the beeswax show through in places.</p>
