<h1>JOSEF MACH</h1>
<h2>Isn&rsquo;t It Cool How Nothing Here Changes at All?</h2>
<p><em>Body Design Studio</em><br><br><strong>Your installation shows the encounter of a clearly structured grid with the&nbsp;material fragility of porcelain. Can we read this as signifying a &ldquo;giant with&nbsp;feet of clay&rdquo; &ndash; that is, a system that appears stable but is in fact built on&nbsp;shaky foundations?</strong><br>Exactly. The grid, represented by a checkerboard pattern, is actually a metaphor for binary structures and prescribed rules that influence and constrain us every day. I wanted to create a landscape that would represent the current state of the world. I have been thinking about how rules and the constant drive to simplification and categorization actually do not help us, and about how a system that ultimately serves only a handful of people is, at its core, fragile.&nbsp;<br>I chose the materials for their unique properties, which I then developed conceptually. We know that wax is soft, that it burns, and that beeswax smells sweet, and we also know that porcelain is a fragile and delicate, almost noble, material. I chose porcelain specifically for its fragility and beauty, but also due to people&rsquo;s fear of handling it. Its untouchability and ubiquity led me to draw a parallel with contemporary patriarchal capitalism, which stands on very fragile foundations.</p>
<p><br><strong>The theme of dysfunction and failure appears in several aspects of your work.&nbsp;Are there situations in which you perceive failure as a productive moment?</strong><br>Here, I would build on the ideas of Meg-John Barker: Meg speaks of failure&nbsp;as a form of resistance against patriarchy and heteronormativity. Failure can&nbsp;become a means of liberation by revealing the mechanisms that limit us. So, yes,&nbsp;failure within a dysfunctional and limiting system can actually be a productive&nbsp;moment. And this can happen even on an emotional or identity-related level.</p>
<p><strong>The motif of the chessboard carries with it the idea of rules and clearly&nbsp;defined positions. What do you think about this system &ndash; do you think it is&nbsp;possible to disrupt or transform its boundaries, or does it always shape us&nbsp;in some way?</strong><br>It certainly shapes us in some way. It is simply something we are thrown&nbsp;into, and we all try not to drown in it. However, I think it is definitely possible&nbsp;to break down the boundaries of the system, and that we are, in a way, all&nbsp;doing it. It is impossible to fully live within these binary structures due to the&nbsp;very nature of holistic existence. It is something that simply does not exist.&nbsp;Take, for example, gender, sexuality, and their social constructs within binary&nbsp;positions &ndash; whether someone is masculine or feminine has nothing to do&nbsp;with gender. Social constructs of the &ldquo;proper man&rdquo; or &ldquo;good woman&rdquo; serve&nbsp;a system that stands on shaky ground and ultimately serves only itself. My&nbsp;own identity is not heteronormative; it is my personal &ldquo;disobedience&rdquo; towards&nbsp;these systemic norms.</p>
