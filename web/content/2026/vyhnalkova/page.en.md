<h1>AD&Eacute;LA VYHN&Aacute;LKOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>ALL MY WORKS ARE LIES...<br>writing as the manifestation of living</h2>
<p><em>Graphic Design Studio 1</em><br><br><strong>For your graduate work, you decided to create a book&hellip; In fact, to write it,&nbsp;too. It seems to me the book is about friendship and is intended for friends,&nbsp;imbued with life experiences that you observe and let impact you. What life&nbsp;milestones led you to writing?</strong><br>Yes, that&rsquo;s right! I think writing was more of a general desire I&rsquo;ve always had, one that gradually merged with other activities. Sometimes, when my parents are cleaning the house I grew up in, they find a little book that I don&rsquo;t even remember creating as a child. So, rather than major turning points, writing was probably about tiny steps for me &ndash; and there have been a whole lot of them.&nbsp;I think the milestones were more about the people I met along the way who&nbsp;influenced how I do things. And that is actually what you are talking about &ndash;&nbsp;friendship and alliances.</p>
<p><strong>Your writing flows freely like speech or an unrestrained stream of inner&nbsp;dialogue. Do you consider yourself a compulsive writer?</strong><br>Haha, well&hellip; Maybe&hellip; You could theoretically call a certain part of my work obsessive. Some form of &ldquo;obsession&rdquo; or deep interest is actually an important part of it, and perhaps even the reason why I am able to engage with things for so long. But what&rsquo;s important is that it is not just about the text, but other themes as well. The ones I write about or associate with the process &ndash; such as walking, communicating, and telepathy with everyone including people, animals, clouds, and the whole earth; the need to be able to take care of myself in all sorts of situations &ndash; in short, the goal of navigating the world honestly and independently. But I don&rsquo;t approach my projects mindlessly or pathologically&nbsp;(in the original sense). Even though they often involve long-term, spontaneous,&nbsp;or monotonous &ndash; even meditative &ndash; activities, I reflect on them critically and&nbsp;strive to be mindful of their progression. In the past, I have sometimes jokingly&nbsp;introduced myself to acquaintances as a &lsquo;professional pyromaniac&rsquo; &ndash; whether&nbsp;it is about starting fires, writing, or anything else, I consciously work with the&nbsp;connection between these fascinations, often with exaggeration and humor.</p>
<p><strong>Is your work about learning to recognize all kinds of life conventions, become&nbsp;aware of them, and, by extension, about trying to escape them?</strong><br>In some cases, yes. It was about finding ways to create a story that, through its&nbsp;content, form, and setting, would best represent the principles I myself would&nbsp;like to live by as both a person and a designer: being outdoors, learning through&nbsp;experience, being skilled and resourceful in designing and making things,&nbsp;and fostering direct, face-to-face connections with others. Over time, I have&nbsp;come to certain decisions through which I strive to fulfill these resolutions of&nbsp;mine. My project often refers to the Tramps, who set themselves apart from&nbsp;certain social conventions &ndash; and yes, sometimes even seek to escape them.</p>
