<h1 class="p1">MAEDEH HOSEINI</h1>
<h2>Where Memory Dwells</h2>
<p><em>Fine Art and Design</em></p>
<p><strong>Your work reveals a hidden window in the gallery by cutting a hole in the&nbsp;wall. How do you perceive this intervention &ndash; as an archeological gesture&nbsp;which gives a place its memory back, or as a disruption of the white cube&nbsp;as a neutral exhibition space?</strong><br>The idea for the cut-out came from a moment of surprise, when I realised&nbsp;that the window visible on the facade of the building, something you may&nbsp;pass by every time in the city centre, has completely lost its connection to&nbsp;the interior space because of the false wall covering it. As my research on&nbsp;displacement and place developed, I became interested in reactivating the&nbsp;primary corporeal experience of this place, which originally happens through&nbsp;its windows and its relation to the street. In that sense, the act of cutting and&nbsp;revealing what once was introduces a feeling of fragmentation associated&nbsp;with displacement. Although the act of disruption can hint at white cube&nbsp;critiques, my main intention was to approach this window as a transitional&nbsp;element, creating a dialogue with its place and displacement, rather than&nbsp;functioning as a neutral exhibition space.</p>
<p><br><strong>In the conclusion, you mention the concept of wrong place as an alternative to both a stable sense of home and to complete placelessness. How&nbsp;do you understand this sense of being in the wrong place in relation to&nbsp;unrootedness, identity, and the possibility of new forms of connection?</strong><br>I think I perceive myself as a transient subject. And I really relate to what Lucy&nbsp;Lippard writes in her amazing book, The Lure of the Local, that displacement&nbsp;is the norm for many of us, and we make connections with places in passing. I would also add people and stories. The range of topics, from a stable&nbsp;sense of home to displacement in the world and time we are living in, can&nbsp;transcend any single perspective and may even appear pathological. I am&nbsp;interested in how the experience of displacement functions as a transition,&nbsp;and as you consciously or inevitably go through it, how you find yourself&nbsp;in the archipelagos of not-being-at-home, but not being totally placeless.</p>
<p><strong>How do you perceive displacement? What fascinates you about the&nbsp;relationship between physical movement and the feeling of never being&nbsp;fully settled?</strong><br>I think of displacement as something intrinsic to habitation itself. Both of&nbsp;my residences back in Iran and here in Brno face each other through their&nbsp;windows. In my work, I interpreted the transitionality and fragmentary nature&nbsp;of displacement, moments where you are no longer coherent but instead feel&nbsp;disoriented and suspended. What interests me is that both being fully settled&nbsp;and being on the move produce forms of belonging. One in stillness and the&nbsp;other through transience and continuous passage. I think we existentially&nbsp;seek belonging, wherever we are.</p>
