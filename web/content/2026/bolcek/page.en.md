<h1>&Scaron;IMON BOLCEK</h1>
<h2>How Life Imitates Film</h2>
<p><em>Painting Studio 1</em><br><br><strong>I feel that the individual images reference various film genres and that for&nbsp;you genre is not merely about a visual style, but rather a specific way of&nbsp;interpreting a situation. Is there a framework you use to assign particular&nbsp;film genres to specific personal experiences?</strong><br>I am glad the images affect you in various ways. For me, the key to choosing&nbsp;a genre is found in the emotions or feelings I am experiencing at that moment&nbsp;with my loved ones, friends, and family. The color palette and composition&nbsp;that build the image&rsquo;s overall atmosphere develop from that.</p>
<p><strong>Movie posters have traditionally been associated with commercialism. As&nbsp;part of your artistic practice, do you perceive it more as a form of &ldquo;low-brow&rdquo;&nbsp;visual language, or do you consciously avoid such a distinction?</strong><br>I certainly don&rsquo;t avoid it, but I naturally view the movie poster as a form&nbsp;whose visual impact is meant to attract people. The airbrush technique is&nbsp;part of this, as it is associated with commercial aesthetics and allows for&nbsp;many interesting effects. So, in a way, I perceive the poster as &ldquo;low-brow,&rdquo;&nbsp;but at the same time I am fascinated by its compositional sophistication,&nbsp;which I try to use and work with in different ways. I strive to find the boundary&nbsp;between the image and the poster.</p>
<p><strong>When I compare your Bachelor&rsquo;s thesis with your Master&rsquo;s thesis, it is&nbsp;hard not to notice that you have shifted significantly from working with&nbsp;self-contained units to a more fragmentary approach. To what extent does&nbsp;this shift represent a fundamental change in your approach to storytelling?</strong><br>I think it is a major shift in the way I tell stories. In my Bachelor&rsquo;s thesis,&nbsp;I worked with paraphrases of historically known paintings, their interpretations, the hierarchy of figures, and context. However, the scene was&nbsp;constructed as a whole. The fragmentary nature of the poster allows me&nbsp;to place figures, still lifes and landscapes within the image and layer them&nbsp;freely on top of one another. The fragments thus provide the viewer with the&nbsp;illusion of a story and function as a collage assembled from various parts&nbsp;of a plotline for a fictional film.</p>
