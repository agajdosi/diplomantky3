<h1>BARBORA PAVLASOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>The Girl Theory</h2>
<p><em>Video Studio</em><br><br><strong>As the title suggests, your project refers to the Theory of the Young-Girl,&nbsp;which aims to describe the phenomenon of a particular subjectivity and&nbsp;the performativity of a self-image we use to present ourselves online and&nbsp;elsewhere. What makes this topic interesting to you? Do you think it is an&nbsp;accurate description of how our identities are formed today?</strong><br>What I enjoy about the Theory of the Young-Girl is that it connects intimate experience with the broader structures of platform capitalism and digital culture.&nbsp;What often seems like a purely personal problem &ndash; the need for self-staging,&nbsp;optimization and constant self-analysis &ndash; is revealed as something systemic.&nbsp;I think it is a fairly accurate description of identity formation today, precisely&nbsp;because the Young-Girl does not represent a fixed type of subject, but&nbsp;something that is constantly changing along with aesthetics, the evolution&nbsp;of platforms, and modes of online performativity.</p>
<p><strong>Does the topic relate to you personally? To what extent is your work&nbsp;autobiographical, or perhaps autofictional?</strong><br>Yes, the topic is personal to me as well. However, my goal was not to create a purely autobiographical account. I use personal experience more as&nbsp;a starting point for reflecting on the broader mechanisms of contemporary&nbsp;visual culture.<br>A certain degree of stylization and construction is important for the project,&nbsp;so if I had to describe my strategy in as few words as possible, I would call&nbsp;it autotheory with a touch of autofiction.</p>
<p><strong>The resulting video features a series of striking images. Long silver nails,&nbsp;a glass cube, and a mirrored phone case all have one thing in common &ndash;&nbsp;they reflect their surroundings. Is this a metaphor and, if yes, what does&nbsp;it mean?</strong><br>Yes, reflection is one of the key motifs of the entire work for me. I am&nbsp;interested in situations where a person begins to perceive themselves&nbsp;primarily through their own representation and thus gradually internalizes&nbsp;the perspective of their surroundings. In the context of the work, reflection&nbsp;functions as a metaphor for contemporary subjectivity, shaped by constant&nbsp;visibility and self-reflection.</p>
