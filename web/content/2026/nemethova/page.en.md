<h1>S&Aacute;RA N&Eacute;METHOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Garden Without Fences</h2>
<p><em>Painting Studio 3</em><br><br><strong>When I compare your older works with your graduate work, I clearly see&nbsp;the motif of nature &ndash; a theme you have been exploring for a long time. But&nbsp;you seem to have shifted from painting toward spatial installations, or&nbsp;has this been just a brief detour?</strong><br>I think that spatial installation is a natural culmination of my work to date.&nbsp;Even in my previous works I worked with the arrangement of pieces in space.&nbsp;I previously related to nature mainly through abstract painting, and my thesis&nbsp;project delves deeper in this direction. It focuses on a specific place and&nbsp;explores the origins of my relationship to it through my family&rsquo;s gardening&nbsp;business. The installation was created from materials sourced directly&nbsp;from there, and I added photography, which is a new form of presentation&nbsp;for me. I currently enjoy working with materials and finding various ways of&nbsp;adjusting them. I believe I have not exhausted all possibilities in this regard,&nbsp;but I will certainly return to painting in the future.</p>
<p><strong>Your central source of inspiration was the family gardening business and&nbsp;your upbringing there. Did it only provide inspiration for working with&nbsp;natural motifs, or has the idea of nostalgia also been important to you?</strong><br>I wouldn&rsquo;t use the word nostalgia, at least I have never thought of it that way.&nbsp;Working with the family gardening business was rather liberating: although&nbsp;nature has always been present in my work, it is only now that I feel I have&nbsp;understood why. It most likely includes a certain element of nostalgia, as&nbsp;I realized my strong connection to the place when I started visiting there&nbsp;more often, perhaps also because it was created around the same time I was&nbsp;born. I first perceived this environment as a playground, later I went there&nbsp;for summer jobs, and nowadays I have been focusing on it through artistic&nbsp;research. The personal aspect is important to me, but understanding the&nbsp;connections is more important than my personal memories.</p>
<p><strong>Gardening is about caring for nature, but it also entails a certain alienation&nbsp;from the original environment: nature is no longer wild, but functions&nbsp;within the boundaries set by people, as a so-called &lsquo;cultivar&rsquo;. How do&nbsp;you understand this?</strong><br>I try to thematize this contradiction in my work, and it provides tension.&nbsp;Although people strive for cultivation, the plants and objects used in gardening possess their own strength and vitality. I am not so much interested&nbsp;in &ldquo;wildness&rdquo; but rather in how these materials affect us and how they&nbsp;interact with each other as part of a common assemblage, whether it is&nbsp;about degrading plastic or a plant seeking a way out of its allotted space.&nbsp;As part of my installation, I do not take these materials from their context&nbsp;in order to control them, but instead give them space to exert their own&nbsp;agency in new forms.</p>
