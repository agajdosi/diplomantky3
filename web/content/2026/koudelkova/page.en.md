<h1>TEREZA KOUDELKOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Planetary Writings</h2>
<p><em>Game Media Studio</em><br><br><strong>Your game gives agency to the character and is more like a collaboration&nbsp;between it and the player. What does this shift mean to you in terms of&nbsp;experience &ndash; is it more about disrupting the player&rsquo;s ability to act, or a way&nbsp;to foster a relationship between the player and the character?</strong><br>I wanted the character to express itself in the game and in the gameplay.&nbsp;This actually brings them up to the level of the player &ndash; they become equal&nbsp;participants in the process. The player is often treated as a nearly divine&nbsp;entity, while in my game this hierarchy is challenged or completely broken.&nbsp;The character instead guides the player, reclaims its body, and sometimes&nbsp;takes back complete control. The player can only control the game by moving&nbsp;the thumbstick &ndash; there is no interaction button. The player performs something and waits for the character; and the character in turn waits patiently&nbsp;for us to figure out the next step. From the perspective of game creation, it&nbsp;would have been way easier to code a button.</p>
<p><strong>You describe the relationship between the player and the avatar as potentially symbiotic. Do you think this relationship can also be conflictual,&nbsp;a clash of two wills sharing one body?</strong><br>Absolutely. It creates a state similar to triggering a game cut-scene. The&nbsp;player is used to fully interacting with the game world, but is suddenly&nbsp;blocked from entering and watches a cinematic scene unfolding. When&nbsp;a person who wants to fully inhabit the character&rsquo;s body plays my game,&nbsp;they will have a problem with the character&rsquo;s actions. Not having a digital&nbsp;body at their disposal and instead having to fight for it with someone else&nbsp;is a kind of unpleasant experience, and that is exactly what I was trying to&nbsp;evoke in this work.</p>
<p><strong>You use traditional art techniques such as ink and watercolor in a digital&nbsp;environment. Is this a way of &ldquo;humanizing&rdquo; digital bodies?</strong><br>It is probably more about humanizing my creative process... I&rsquo;ve always&nbsp;worked in a more traditional way and I sometimes have difficulty with digital&nbsp;games: everything is somehow intangible and untouchable, you can&rsquo;t hold&nbsp;things in your hand. Although 3D modeling and animation are fascinating,&nbsp;it is very different from illustration or a board game. I&rsquo;ve struggled with that&nbsp;in my previous projects, and texturing using non-digital techniques is a way&nbsp;for me to work with this. I am not only humanizing the body but probably&nbsp;everything around it. I am also trying to digitalize the artistic technique itself&nbsp;by layering, blending, and salting, which is harder because the zero value of&nbsp;paper is white, while the zero value of the screen is black; and there&rsquo;s also&nbsp;the possibility of a manual error. But that touch is actually a central theme&nbsp;for the whole project (and many of my fingerprints are indeed visible &ndash; please&nbsp;don&rsquo;t look for them).</p>
