<h1>&Scaron;TĚP&Aacute;NKA TRČKOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Landscapes</h2>
<p><em>Game Media Studio</em><br><br><strong>Your graduate exhibition presents a scenography made of mobile scenery&nbsp;which played a part in the workshop preceding the exhibition: what was&nbsp;the workshop about and what did you have the participants do?</strong><br>During the workshop, the participants were given reference photographs&nbsp;of the landscape, which I used as a basis for creating the backdrops and&nbsp;scenes. The photographs recorded the time and camera settings which the&nbsp;participants could use as a point of departure for their photography. The&nbsp;entire scene had dynamic lighting which changed from morning to evening&nbsp;light over the course of a single hour.<br>The participants therefore manipulated the backdrops and explored different compositions and the behavior of shapes under changing lighting&nbsp;conditions. I left it up to them whether they would be inspired by the photographs or just play with the scene and visuals.</p>
<p><br><strong>Light plays a key role in your thinking. For me, this is something that connects&nbsp;your interest in photography with the theater scene, which the installation&nbsp;may resemble, and also with game design (or at least 3D graphics) which you&nbsp;studied at FFA. Is that a correct assumption? How are these fields related?</strong><br>Yes, rather than a theatrical scene, I see it as a stylized environment that&nbsp;allowed people to observe similarities with a real landscape that had been&nbsp;transferred from reality into a scene, and to be further inspired by it. This&nbsp;also happens in game design, where the landscape is usually modelled as&nbsp;realistically as possible, but the space&rsquo;s atmosphere and personal impressions are often neglected.</p>
<p><strong>How important is the installation&rsquo;s interactive aspect for you?</strong><br>Making the scene interactive gave the participants the opportunity to&nbsp;perceive it in different ways. Personally, it was interesting for me to watch&nbsp;how each group of participants worked with the composition in a different&nbsp;way and saw different things in it. Some of the compositional solutions&nbsp;the participants came up with had not occurred to me before, which was&nbsp;enriching. It was also stimulating to watch how the participants collaborated&nbsp;in creating the composition and photographs together, and how they shared&nbsp;their visions and experiences with photography.</p>
