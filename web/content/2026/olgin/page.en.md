<h1>DMITRII OLGIN</h1>
<h2>Anonymous Hospitality</h2>
<p><em>Intermedia Studio</em><br><br><strong>The project <em>Anonymous Hospitality</em> works with the concept of the crazy&nbsp;archive. What exactly does this term mean? And is there a way to update&nbsp;it for the present day?</strong><br><em>Crazy archive </em>is a concept I borrow from artist Agnieszka Polska, and it&nbsp;describes a specific state of memory in the post-socialist context &ndash; an environment where historical material exists as a blend of memories, fabrications,&nbsp;and reconstructions. It consists in the life of the archive which, however,&nbsp;never actually existed in the form it is presented in. Amid the current global&nbsp;crises, fake news, and conspiracy theories, its instability becomes chaotic.&nbsp;And the fear of this totality is paralyzing. That is why we must move from&nbsp;attempting to interpret the archive to encountering it as the Other.</p>
<p><br><strong>The archive you built deals with the theme of a visit. You perceive hospitality, welcoming the other into one&rsquo;s home, as a kind of ritual. What&nbsp;fascinates you about the performative aspect of visits?</strong><br>A feast is a unique situation that disrupts the humdrum of everyday life. It&nbsp;is a scene composed of random moments &ndash; a carnival where the past rubs&nbsp;shoulders with the present, the familiar with the foreign &ndash; and all of this&nbsp;works with traditions of hospitality. The external does not match the internal;&nbsp;roles shift; behind the usual order of things is the possibility of something&nbsp;strange. Ancient Greek mythology includes myths about an unexpected&nbsp;guest whose identity always eludes us. The host must offer the stranger&nbsp;a place at the table; he must not reveal his identity, for sometimes it turns&nbsp;out that he is a spirit, a god in disguise, or a deceased relative. The stranger&nbsp;on the threshold represents the clash of the ordinary with the extraordinary.<br>The archive requires a similar approach &ndash; without any attempt to definitively&nbsp;identify or organize it. Here, the performative aspect of hospitality makes&nbsp;the ritual of the feast itself a form of memory. It is not about recording an&nbsp;event, but about its reenactment.</p>
<p><strong>The dog passport occupies a special place in your project. Why is it&nbsp;so important?</strong><br>Through the story of obtaining an EU dog passport, the guest&rsquo;s otherness&nbsp;takes on a bureaucratic dimension. This veterinary document often makes&nbsp;it easier to cross borders for dogs than for people from &ldquo;third countries.&rdquo; The&nbsp;figure of the Other in the form of a dog-man is more mobile and anonymous.&nbsp;It draws on the image of St. Christopher &ndash; the patron saint of travelers&nbsp;with a dog&rsquo;s head. According to one legend, he came from the land of the&nbsp;Cynocephali &ndash; a dog-headed people deprived of the gift of human speech.&nbsp;According to another, he was handsome from birth but prayed for disfigurement to avoid temptation. According to a third version, his appearance&nbsp;was merely a symbol of pagan brutality.</p>
