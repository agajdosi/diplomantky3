<h1>KARYA &Ouml;NER</h1>
<h2>The Self as Heterotopia</h2>
<p><em>Fine Art and Design</em></p>
<p><strong><em>The Self as Heterotopia</em> reflects on Michel Foucault&rsquo;s eponymous concept, depicting a &ldquo;space within a space&rdquo; which offers an alternative to&nbsp;its original environment while simultaneously reflecting its surroundings.&nbsp;How does collage enable you to approach and develop this concept?</strong><br>Collage has always been a therapeutic method for me. Its inherent limitations paradoxically create a sense of freedom, allowing me to work within&nbsp;constraints while still exploring open-ended possibilities. This project began with a shift in my thinking: rather than focusing on a fixed goal, I was&nbsp;more interested in the intuitive process. I used fragments to build spatial<br>relationships, and the work evolved organically. In relation to Foucault&rsquo;s concept of heterotopia, these spaces operate through different systems and&nbsp;hierarchies than those of the external world. As an eclectic and inherently&nbsp;fragmentary technique, collage formally reflects this. Its discontinuities,&nbsp;juxtapositions, and layered structures mirror the heterogeneous nature&nbsp;of heterotopic space.</p>
<p><strong>The original visual sources of your collages are fragmented to such an extent that I have trouble recognizing them. What motifs are you working with and is their origin important for you?</strong><br>My earlier work used a mixed media approach to combine found images with&nbsp;paint, deconstructing them to create painterly compositions. The images&rsquo;&nbsp;origins were not important to me then. For my graduate project, however,&nbsp;I started using my own photographs. The imagery is still fragmented, but&nbsp;a bit more visible and traceable. The photographs come from places that<br>are personally meaningful to me and I aimed to reconstruct them into layered, heterotopic atmospheres. I also worked with plexiglass, emphasizing&nbsp;their fleeting nature &ndash; moments that pass in front of us but cannot be fully&nbsp;grasped. I try to evoke a more collective sense of something close to us, yet&nbsp;always slightly out of reach, almost like an illusion.</p>
<p><strong>Using plexiglass and video projection provided your work with elements&nbsp;of transparency and reflection, and made it a complex installation. Did&nbsp;this help you discover some new aspects in it?</strong><br>Using these materials and media allowed me to think of the work as a single,&nbsp;unified piece rather than separate elements. I wanted everything to come&nbsp;together&ndash; moving images and still images coexisting and interacting. The&nbsp;video also includes layered, collage-like sequences and, along with the&nbsp;plexiglass, it creates shifting atmospheres and colors. Transparent surfaces&nbsp;introduce reflections and overlaps, extending the collage into the surrounding environment. The installation format helped me see my practice more&nbsp;holistically, rather than focusing on individual aspects. This approach aligns&nbsp;more closely with my aim to create an immersive environment.</p>
