<h1>KATEŘINA PROCH&Aacute;ZKOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>I Play Pok&eacute;mon Go Every Day</h2>
<p><em>Drawing and Printmaking Studio</em><br><br><strong>Your work focuses on the phenomenon of collecting, along with many other&nbsp;interesting topics &ndash; the transformation of symbolic value into exchange&nbsp;value, self-identification, and so on. But I cannot help but ask you a personal&nbsp;question: do you collect Pok&eacute;mon cards? And do you really play Pok&eacute;mon&nbsp;Go every day?</strong><br>I approach Pok&eacute;mon cards more from the perspective of a third parent. They&nbsp;serve as a common topic for communication between me and my stepson.&nbsp;I sometimes feel bad about constantly buying more Pok&eacute;mon packs, but I also&nbsp;realize that they provide a shared topic of conversation at home, like which type&nbsp;of Pok&eacute;mon is best, the most expensive, and what can be traded with classmates. I sometimes see people in Luž&aacute;nky, aged ten to forty, staring at their&nbsp;phones and occasionally tripping over something. They are playing Pok&eacute;mon Go,&nbsp;catching Pok&eacute;mon: they are walking through the park, but perceive it through&nbsp;the virtual world of Pok&eacute;mon, which I find fascinating. My project is another&nbsp;layer of a fictional world where I have the Pok&eacute;mon I made battle real social&nbsp;problems. In this sense, it is true that I Play Pok&eacute;mon Go Every Day.</p>
<p><strong>Your comparison of the Pok&eacute;mon concept (training, upgrading, and evolution) with the topic of biopolitics very interesting. Can you speak more&nbsp;about this?</strong><br>My take on biopolitics naturally draws on Michel Foucault, who describes how&nbsp;modern power governs our lives &ndash; our bodies, health, reproduction, and performance. And this is precisely where Pok&eacute;mon begin to function as a model &ndash; they&nbsp;are constantly evaluated, trained, and improved to achieve peak performance.&nbsp;I also draw on posthumanist thought, because I perceive Pok&eacute;mon as hybrid&nbsp;entities straddling the line between organism, technology, and fiction. It is&nbsp;about transforming how we understand the body and identity, and how these&nbsp;categories are shaped by the system and our imagination.</p>
<p><strong>What traits did you assign the Pok&eacute;mon you created, and how did you&nbsp;develop their fictional world?</strong><br>I assigned my own Pok&eacute;mon traits that are more psychological and social in nature. Each of them represents a certain type of experience or power &ndash; the experience of queer people, of faith, the pressure of normativity and power mechanisms. Just like the franchise characters, my fictional creatures are defined by type, power, and evolution, and each of them has particular moves that respond to specific situations.&nbsp;<br><em>Aura Drift </em>reacts to expressions of hatred toward queer people, resisting&nbsp;heteronormative subjects and providing a defense mechanism for other&nbsp;Pok&eacute;mon from the LGBTQ+ group. My fictional creatures also include <em>Sanctilume</em> (a mechanism of faith), <em>Glowli</em> (a hallucinogen, a personal protective&nbsp;mechanism, empathy, and shared vulnerability), and Grawemarch (a heteronormative, masculine character).</p>
