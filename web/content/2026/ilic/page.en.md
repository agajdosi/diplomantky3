<h1>ANDREJ ILIČ</h1>
<h2>Back In Shape</h2>
<p><em>Product Design Studio</em><br><br><strong>Your work develops from materially-oriented design: you did not determine&nbsp;the outcome you wanted to achieve in your project beforehand. At what&nbsp;point did your experiment with aluminum foam turn into a concrete product &ndash; the electric heater? You also mention in the text that some failures&nbsp;were crucial to your research. Was there a moment during the process&nbsp;that fundamentally changed the direction of the entire design?</strong><br>The key moment for me was the phase when I acquired a detailed understanding of the behavior of molten aluminum. I really &ldquo;got a sense&rdquo; for the&nbsp;material. After a series of ten castings, I managed to create a truly large cast&nbsp;of aluminum foam by using a systematic approach. At that moment, I knew&nbsp;I could embark on something bigger. I partially submerged a cast cube&nbsp;(10 cm) of aluminum foam in hot water and was captivated by how quickly&nbsp;the heat permeated the material. But what was even more interesting was&nbsp;that after a few minutes, the water cooled so rapidly that I realized heat&nbsp;transfer would be the right &ldquo;use case.&rdquo; This also confirmed the information&nbsp;from my original research.</p>
<p><strong>As part of your process, you use more accessible and experimental methods of producing aluminum foam instead of processes that would be more&nbsp;industrially demanding. Do you think about the potential of democratizing&nbsp;design technology and the possibility of manufacturing more advanced&nbsp;products away from industrial facilities?</strong><br>This is exactly the approach I am using in my thesis. And it works! In other&nbsp;words, we live in an age when we have so much information and instruction&nbsp;right at our fingertips. High-tech methods are quickly becoming commonplace and accessible. That opens up new possibilities. So why not give it&nbsp;a try? When I started this project, I had absolutely no idea if I would succeed&nbsp;or where it would lead. It takes a lot of work, a lot of sketching and thinking.&nbsp;But the outcome was worth the effort.</p>
<p><strong>If you decided to continue developing the project, would you be interested&nbsp;in delving into production technology in more detail, or would you prefer&nbsp;to explore new product possibilities for this material?</strong><br>If I had an investor, I would love to explore the limits of the aluminum foam&nbsp;casting process I have designed. I think that would be really interesting.&nbsp;However, this is less likely, so I am focusing on finding new ways to make even&nbsp;better use of the existing material. Since aluminum isn&rsquo;t exactly a traditional&nbsp;material in this field, there are truly many paths to explore.</p>
