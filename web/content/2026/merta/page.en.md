<h1>ONDŘEJ MERTA</h1>
<h2>Build a fire that&rsquo;s just right &mdash; not too big, not too small</h2>
<p><em>Painting Studio 2</em></p>
<p><br><strong>Your work draws on personal and family experiences, yet it also raises&nbsp;broader questions about representation. Does indirectness provide you&nbsp;a way to maintain your own perspective while avoiding the idea that it is&nbsp;a statement on behalf of an entire community?</strong><br>For me, indirectness leaves room for ambivalence, doubt, as well as a personal dimension. I do not have to translate everything into an unambiguous&nbsp;statement or stance. At the same time, I feel that some things can be&nbsp;formulated more precisely with a metaphor, a situation or a material, rather&nbsp;than taking a direct approach. I never try to create an image of collective&nbsp;experience. That is not even possible.</p>
<p><strong>You have been working along with your mother for a long time. Has this collaboration evolved over time from a working method into something more fundamental to your very thinking about your work? If so, in what way?&nbsp;</strong><br>It was never just a working method. I initially saw this collaboration as an opportunity to create a shared space within which we could create a specific work. However, I gradually realized that the very process of this collaboration is perhaps more important than the resulting work.<br>I would say that for my mother, it was always mainly about spending time&nbsp;together. The resulting works have actually been secondary to that. And&nbsp;I feel the same way.<br>Conversations and the revisiting of various situations started to function&nbsp;as a way to reflect on the transmission of experience in general &ndash; not just&nbsp;what is being passed on, but especially the conditions under which such&nbsp;transmission can take place.</p>
<p><strong>How important do you consider the origins and previous lives of the&nbsp;objects you use in your installations? Do you perceive your work as&nbsp;readymades, or rather as situated work with objects?</strong><br>A certain coherence is important to me &ndash; the feeling that everything&nbsp;somehow fits together, even on levels that are not immediately apparent.&nbsp;Information about the origin of the objects and materials or their previous&nbsp;life is not so important to me, but I rather reflect that they carry a certain&nbsp;experience or relationship to other parts of the work. I often feel that the&nbsp;installation functions more as a structure of relationships than as a collection of individual things.</p>
