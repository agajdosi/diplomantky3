<h1>TEREZIE TOMKOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Atlas of Small Joys</h2>
<p><em>Painting Studio 3</em><br><br><strong>In Atlas of Small Joys, you build a monument to small, seemingly unnecessary objects that may, however, have immeasurable personal value for&nbsp;their owner. What drove you to make ceramic copies of them?</strong><br>Ceramic versions of small things allow us to return to the moment of their&nbsp;discovery. I enjoy how ordinary and almost invisible things begin transforming into small stories and memories, and rouse the imagination.</p>
<p><strong>I can&rsquo;t help but ask; I have drawers and boxes full of these things at home&nbsp;and sometimes I wish I was more decisive and just threw away the junk.&nbsp;How about you? Do you just uncritically love your little trinkets?</strong><br>I would like to have fewer things around me, but that&rsquo;s difficult as an artist&nbsp;and a teacher. Furthermore, I have in recent months started considering&nbsp;my room more as a studio, so found objects, materials, and work materials&nbsp;have slowly been settling there.</p>
<p><strong>How important is the question of spirituality to you? Do you attach any&nbsp;magical meaning to these little things? Are they talismans?</strong><br>Sensitivity to ordinary moments and subtle things were very important to&nbsp;me in this work. I consider these small objects as vessels of time that retain&nbsp;traces of a situation, mood or brief moment that would otherwise disappear.&nbsp;For me, the work also had a therapeutic aspect.</p>
