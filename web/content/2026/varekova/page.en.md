<h1>MARIE VAŘEKOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Weary Shout</h2>
<p><em>Painting Studio 3</em><br><br><strong>As part of your graduate work, you present images and objects that thematically address today&rsquo;s information and sensory overload, which we&nbsp;traditionally associate mainly with the digital sphere. What advantages&nbsp;do traditional media provide?</strong><br>I do not think the two are mutually exclusive. You cannot escape the experience of a highly algorithmicized society, even when working with so-called&nbsp;&ldquo;traditional&rdquo; media. The paintings to a certain extent mimic the degradation&nbsp;of visual information, as we know it from the online environment &ndash; like blurring, compression, or the image&rsquo;s gradual disintegration or even complete&nbsp;breakdown. They reflect the influence of algorithmic flows and generative&nbsp;codes, the experience of our extensive online existence, and their impact&nbsp;on emotions. Painting as a medium has a long historical context, one which&nbsp;has been significantly impacted by the technological revolution of Web&nbsp;2.0. I therefore believe that painting does not stand apart from the digital&nbsp;world, but arises within conditions in which the internet and algorithms have&nbsp;become the infrastructure of everyday life and have had a fundamental&nbsp;social impact.</p>
<p><strong>But you also work with puppet-like objects. How did you come to work&nbsp;with them?</strong><br>Although I avoided puppets while studying my BA degree in puppetry,&nbsp;I have come full circle and returned to them in my latest work. I draw on my&nbsp;experience with the puppetry craft and its tradition. The displayed puppet&nbsp;is highly stylized and has an expressive face. It is not any specific personification, but it does reflect my interest in various mythological creatures&nbsp;(although it is not based on any specific religious system). It rather bears&nbsp;traces of my own imagination.</p>
<p><strong>Is intuition important to your work? Do you work deliberately, or do you&nbsp;let the process itself guide you?</strong><br>Intuition and chance play a major role in my work. I don&rsquo;t construct narratives; I am not interested in storytelling, but I prioritize the moment over any&nbsp;premeditated structure. Instead of building a narrative, I prefer immediate,&nbsp;perhaps even raw, expressive forms that arise from impulse rather than&nbsp;deliberation &ndash; though this doesn&rsquo;t preclude structure, meticulousness,&nbsp;and precision. I have long been fascinated by the question of how to communicate to the viewer a process that, in my view, carries elements of the&nbsp;sacred and the mystical. Although it may sound somewhat melodramatic,&nbsp;spirituality and mystery are deeply present in my work. However, I realize&nbsp;that this is a realm that is, at its core, very fragile and difficult to convey,&nbsp;and this compels me to seek ways to talk about it. Sometimes to the point&nbsp;of being unbearable.</p>
