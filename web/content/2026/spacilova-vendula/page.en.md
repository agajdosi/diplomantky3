<h1>VENDULA SP&Aacute;ČILOV&Aacute;</h1>
<h2>Once Upon A Thread</h2>
<p><em>Photography Studio</em><br><br><strong>Your graduate work consists of a photographic editorial for a fashion&nbsp;collection which you yourself worked on. What was your collaboration&nbsp;with designer Zuzana Hliněn&aacute; like?</strong><br>This is not the first time I have collaborated with Zuzana &ndash; we have been&nbsp;collaborating naturally for several years, and this work was an extension of&nbsp;that. We are on the same wavelength both personally and creatively, and&nbsp;often have a similar vision, so the whole process worked very naturally. We&nbsp;knew from the beginning that we wanted to work with 1980s aesthetics, and&nbsp;we gradually focused on power dressing. I mostly provided visual research,&nbsp;photography and experiments with AI, while Zuzana worked out the design&nbsp;of the collection, the silhouette and its distinctive elements. For me, the&nbsp;most valuable thing was the way our approaches connected &ndash; it was not&nbsp;just about photographing finished models, but about jointly creating the&nbsp;entire visual concept from scratch.</p>
<p><strong>How would you describe the relationship between fashion and fashion&nbsp;photography? After having tried out both, has your approach to this&nbsp;relationship changed in any way?</strong><br>Fashion and fashion photography are inseparable for me and they shape&nbsp;one another. I have long been involved in fashion photography, and the&nbsp;longer I have worked in the field, the more I realize its essential role in the&nbsp;wider fashion world. Photography is not just a means of presentation; it can&nbsp;also give clothing additional meanings, atmosphere, even its own story. The&nbsp;editorial often determines the collection&rsquo;s ultimate impact. I am also fascinated by the collective nature of fashion photography &ndash; it is not just about&nbsp;the relationship between the designer and the photographer, but about the&nbsp;cooperation of a creative team that connects different approaches into one&nbsp;whole. Joint creation is one of the most inspiring parts of the whole process.</p>
<p><strong>Your work addresses the phenomenon of &ldquo;power dressing&rdquo;. What does this&nbsp;mean? Its roots go back to the 1980s, so do you think it is still relevant today?</strong><br>Power dressing originated in the 80s as a symbol of female emancipation,&nbsp;self-confidence and women&rsquo;s new position in public and work spaces.&nbsp;Expressive silhouettes, sharp shoulders and suits worked not only in the&nbsp;aesthetic sense, but also as a way of communicating authority, strength,&nbsp;and ambition. Such fashion helped women assert themselves in an environment that had predominantly been male. The historical context of power&nbsp;dressing is fascinating. Its current meaning is not the same &ndash; contemporary&nbsp;fashion is more casual and power dressing often functions as an aesthetic&nbsp;or artistic element. Nevertheless, it remains relevant precisely because it&nbsp;can still express strength, identity and the transformation of social status,&nbsp;albeit in more contemporary language.</p>
